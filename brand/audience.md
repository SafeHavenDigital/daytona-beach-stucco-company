## Q1 — Current Situation `[OWNER]`

**Confirmed 2026-08-30.**

> The prospect has a stucco-related problem and is looking for a solution. They
> may not yet know exactly what is causing the problem or what type of repair or
> service they need. They are contacting a stucco company because they want
> someone with expertise to evaluate the situation and help determine the
> appropriate solution.

### The load-bearing implication

**They arrive undiagnosed, and they know it.**

They are not shopping a known scope. They cannot brief the job, so they cannot
meaningfully compare quotes — which is precisely why a low patch bid and a
correct full-scope bid look like the same product to them at intake.

This is the single most important fact in this file. It means:

- The first thing of value we can give is **a name for the problem**, not a price.
- Diagnosis is not a step *before* the sale — for this buyer it *is* the offer.
  This is why the Stucco Moisture Check works as a paid product rather than a
  loss-leader inspection.
- Any lead magnet that assumes the reader already knows what's wrong with their
  wall will miss. The magnet must work for someone who only knows *something
  looks wrong.*

## Q2 — What They Have Already Tried `[GAP]`

**OPEN GAP. Owner has no verified answer as of 2026-08-30.**

Do not fill this from assumption. Do not write copy that claims what the
prospect already tried.

### What is inferable, and how far it goes `[SERP]`

The owner-supplied PAA set contains *"Can a handyman do stucco repair?"*, which
`keyword-plan.md:547` calls "the most on-positioning PAA question in the set."
`positioning.md:281` independently names the primary alternative as: *hire the
cheapest local patcher, have a painter or handyman caulk and paint over it, or
do nothing until an inspection forces the issue.*

That establishes a **cheaper-labor path is actively considered.** It does **not**
establish whether a given caller has already taken it.

### The specific unknown that matters most

**What share of callers are on their second repair?**

The starred positioning angle — "a patch over a wet wall is a repair you pay for
twice" (`positioning.md:13`) — assumes returning cracks are common in the actual
call log. That assumption is currently **unverified**. It is well-supported as a
*market* claim by the Stage 3 low-trust finding, but it is not confirmed as a
*caller* pattern.

**To close this gap, the owner needs to answer:** Of the last ~20 callers, roughly
how many had already paid someone to fix this same wall before?

## Q3 — What They Believe About the Problem `[SERP]`

Sourced from the owner-supplied PAA set (10 questions → 4 answer-intents,
`keyword-plan.md:538-547`) and live SERP composition. **Directional. Derived from
search behavior, not from surveying actual callers.**

| Belief in play | Evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| *"This might just be cosmetic — maybe a handyman can do it."* | Owner PAA: *Can a handyman do stucco repair?* | They may not know a wall can be wet behind an intact-looking surface. |
| *"I don't know if this is serious or normal."* | `hairline cracks in stucco when to worry` rated `~high` volume, rich PAA (`keyword-plan.md:97,130`) | The core anxiety is **triage**, not repair. They want to know whether to panic. |
| *"Maybe insurance covers this."* | Owner PAA: *Will insurance pay for stucco repair? / Does homeowners insurance cover stucco cracks?* | Signals they expect a number large enough to be worth insuring against. |
| *"There might be water back there, but I can't tell."* | Owner PAA: *How do I know if water is behind my stucco? / Does moisture...* | The suspicion of hidden water **already exists and is unresolved.** The Moisture Check answers a question they are already asking. |
| *"All stucco cracks — everyone says so."* | `[POS]` Stage 3 market, saturated coastal-climate messaging (`positioning.md:288`) | Competitors' own climate messaging has taught this buyer to normalize cracking, which suppresses urgency. |

### Market sophistication `[POS]`

**Stage 3 — mechanism required** (`positioning.md:278`).

Buyers have seen many stucco ads and hold low trust. Superiority claims ("best,"
"quality," "reliable") no longer register. The market must be told *how* and
*why* this works when the last repair didn't.

**Consequence for any lead magnet:** it must show a mechanism. A reassurance-only
asset will not land with this audience.

### Awareness level

**Problem-aware, solution-unaware.** They know something is wrong with the wall
(`[OWNER]` Q1) but do not know what service resolves it. They are not yet
product-aware — most do not know a paid diagnostic is a purchasable thing.

## Q4 — What Makes Them Say "This Is Exactly What I Needed" `[GAP]`

**OPEN GAP. Owner has no verified answer as of 2026-08-30.**

This is the hook question for the entire lead magnet, and it is unanswered. No
substitute exists in SERP data — search queries reveal what people *ask*, never
what made them *feel understood*.

**Best available proxy `[OWNER]`+`[SERP]`, to be treated as hypothesis only:**
Because they arrive undiagnosed and their live questions are triage questions
("when to worry," "how do I know if water is behind it"), the moment of
recognition is *plausibly* the one where a stranger tells them **which kind of
problem they have** and therefore whether to worry. This is consistent with the
voice profile's stated target feeling — relief, "replaces a vague fear with a
specific plan" (`voice-profile.md`).

**Flagged as hypothesis, not fact.** Any lead magnet built before this gap closes
is testing this hypothesis, and should be described that way to the client.

**To close this gap, the owner needs to answer:** What do callers actually say on
the phone when they relax? What sentence makes them stop shopping?

## Caller Mix / Segments `[OWNER]`

**Confirmed 2026-08-30.** The business wants leads across **all** relevant
customer types. **Do not narrow the overall business audience to one group.**

1. Residential homeowners / property owners
2. Commercial property owners
3. Realtors / real estate professionals
4. Condo and HOA boards
5. Property managers

### Segment detail, where `positioning.md` already supports it

| Segment | Source | What's known | Decision driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Residential homeowner** | `[POS]` line 319 | Visible cracking, staining, bubbling paint, soft spots, failed 4-point or pre-sale inspection, insurance or HOA letter. "Already been burned or afraid of being burned." | Fear of paying twice |
| **Beachside / barrier-island owner** | `[POS]` line 361 | Daytona Beach Shores, Ormond Beach, Ponce Inlet, New Smyrna. Also new-build and renovation buyers. | Wants it done right the first time |
| **Realtor / real estate pro** | `[OWNER]`; `[POS]` line 319 references pre-sale inspection | Pre-sale and pre-close stucco findings. **Deadline-driven.** | A closing date |
| **Condo / HOA board** | `[POS]` lines 382-384 | Volusia milestone inspection Phase 2 findings, SIRS obligations, insurer-mandated repairs. **Decision by committee.** Larger contract values, very low competition. | Liability and a legal date |
| **Commercial property owner / manager** | `[OWNER]`; `[POS]` line 382 | Same deadline structure as condo boards. | Managing liability, not shopping craftsmanship |

### The structural split every asset must respect `[POS]`

There are **two fundamentally different buyers** here, not five variations of one:

- **Individual decision, emotional, fear-of-being-burned.** Homeowner, beachside
  owner, and (with a clock attached) realtor.
- **Committee decision, unemotional, deadline-and-liability.** Condo/HOA board,
  property manager, commercial owner. `positioning.md:382` is explicit: *"A
  completely different buyer from the homeowner."* And `:384`: *"not shopping on
  price or craftsmanship — they are managing liability and a date. Whoever makes
  the deadline feel handled wins."*

**Consequence:** a single lead magnet is unlikely to serve both well. Expect the
concept set to either pick one lane deliberately or offer parallel versions.

### Live constraint on the commercial segments ⚠️

`assets.md` records the `milestone-inspection-stucco-repairs` brief as **blocked
on commercial credentials.** `positioning.md` confirms the company is licensed
and insured but supplies **no license number, carrier, or coverage amount**, and
forbids inventing or placeholder-ing any of them.

Committee buyers evaluate credentials as a gating item. Any asset aimed at
condo/HOA/commercial must be built so it does not depend on displaying
credentials we do not have. **This is a live constraint, not a future one.**

## Hard Copy Constraints Carried Forward

These bind every asset written for this audience.

1. **No invented credentials.** No license number, carrier, policy number, or
   coverage amount — not even as a placeholder to be filled later
   (`positioning.md`). Omit the element instead.
2. **No unfilled `{PLACEHOLDER}` tokens may ship** (`voice-profile.md`). Get the
   real number or cut the sentence.
3. **Do not lead with saturated claims** (`positioning.md:283-289`): "licensed and
   insured," "free estimate," "quality workmanship," "X+ years," "residential and
   commercial," and now "salt air and humidity are hard on stucco."
4. **Never conflate the free estimate with the paid Stucco Moisture Check**
   ($300–$500). `positioning.md` marks this a mandatory separation.
5. **The Moisture Check is not a formal independent home inspection report.** Do
   not let any asset imply it substitutes for one.
6. **Stage 3 market:** show mechanism, not adjectives.
