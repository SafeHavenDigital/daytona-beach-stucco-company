# Email 8: 90-Day Reminder

## Subject Line Variants

### A: "Time to measure that crack again"
Plain instruction. Clear, does the job.

### B: "90 days — has it moved?" — recommended
Poses the exact question the reader has been carrying for three months. Short,
specific, and the answer requires walking outside — which is the entire
desired action.

### C: "Your second measurement"
Neutral and calm. Weakest of the three at prompting action.

**Recommended A/B test:** B vs A
**Reason:** Tests question-framing against instruction-framing on a long-delay
re-engagement send, where open rates are hardest.

## Email Copy

Ninety days ago you filled in a Wall Record. This is the reminder.

Go measure the same crack, at the same spot, and write the number next to the
first one.

Take a second photo too, straight on, with something for scale. Same angle as
last time if you can manage it — comparison photos are far more useful than
either photo alone.

**What the comparison tells you:**

**No measurable change.** Good news, and the most common outcome. The crack is
stable. Keep the record, measure again in six months or after a major storm.

**Slightly wider — under about half a millimetre.** Worth watching more
closely than before. Move to a three-month cycle rather than six.

**Noticeably wider, longer, or now with staining or bubbling paint that wasn't
there in the spring.** That's movement, and it's changed category. Reply and
tell me what you're seeing.

**New cracks in new places, or the same crack now showing inside the house.**
Don't wait on this one.

Here's the thing worth appreciating: you now know something no single visit can
establish. Whether a crack is *moving* is a different question from whether a
crack *exists*, and it's usually the more important one. Any contractor who
looks at your wall next year is getting one frame. You've got two.

If the number changed, reply with both measurements and roughly where on the
house it is. Happy to tell you whether it's worth doing anything about — and
if it isn't, I'll say so.

— Daytona Beach Stucco Company
