## Subject Line Variants

### A: "Why the crack came back" — recommended
Names the experience directly. If it's happened to them, this is impossible
not to open. If it hasn't, it's still a straightforward question.

### B: "The repair you pay for twice"
The positioning line itself. Strong, but more sales-flavored — better suited
to the landing page than to a teaching email.

### C: "Same crack, same spot, 14 months later"
Specific and vivid. ⚠️ **Not recommended:** the specificity implies a real
customer case we cannot source. See Open Gaps.

## Email Copy

A pattern worth understanding, because it's the reason this company works the
way it does.

Someone gets a crack repaired. The contractor grinds it out, fills it, textures
it, paints it. It looks genuinely good — better than before. Everyone's
satisfied.

Somewhere between a few months and a couple of years later, the crack is back.
Same wall. Often the same line.

Here's the mechanism.

If the wall behind the stucco was **wet** when that patch went on, nothing
about the patch addressed it. The moisture is still in there. It's still moving
through the sheathing and the framing, still expanding and contracting with
temperature, still doing whatever it was already doing. The patch didn't stop
any of that. It sealed the visible symptom and, in some cases, made drying
slightly harder — because the one path that water had *out* of the wall was
the crack.

A patch over a wet wall isn't a repair. It's a lid.

And this is where it gets expensive. The second failure is almost never the
same size as the first. The months between repairs are months the water kept
working on the lath, the paper, and the framing. The first job might have been
a patch. The second one can be a section of wall opened up, sheathing replaced,
framing evaluated.

So the actual question at the start isn't *how much to fix this crack.* It's
**is there water in this wall right now.**

Those are different questions with different prices, and only one of them can
be answered by looking.

If you're getting quotes: ask each contractor how they'll determine whether
there's moisture behind the wall before they close it up. The answer tells you
a lot. There's a right answer, and "we'll see when we open it" isn't quite it —
by then you've already bought the job.

**Has this happened to you?** If you've paid for a stucco repair that came
back, reply and tell me. It's genuinely useful to know how common it is around
here.

— Daytona Beach Stucco Company
