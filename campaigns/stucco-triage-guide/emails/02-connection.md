## Email Copy

Something you may have noticed about the triage guide:

Three of its four patterns end with some version of *you don't need to do
anything about this right now.*

That's deliberate, and it's worth explaining why, because it's the same reason
our quotes sometimes look different from the other ones you'll get.

The ordinary way to price stucco repair is to look at the wall, see a crack,
and quote the patch. It's fast. The homeowner gets a number the same day.
Everybody's happy right up until the crack comes back — and when it comes back
in the same place, it's usually because the patch sealed something in rather
than fixing it.

So we'd rather find out first, and we'd rather tell you when the answer is
"nothing." A guide that routed every single reader toward a paid service would
be a sales funnel wearing a lab coat. You'd have known. Those are easy to spot.

There's a version of this that costs us money, and we do it anyway: Pattern 3
in the guide — the wide, stepped, or offset cracks — points you toward a
*structural engineer*, not toward us. If a wall is moving because of what's
underneath it, measuring moisture is the wrong first question. We'd be taking
your money to answer something that isn't the problem.

Next email is the one people find most useful: what's actually behind a stucco
wall, and why the outside of it tells you so much less than you'd think.

— Daytona Beach Stucco Company
