# Email 1: Delivery

## Subject Line Variants

### A: "Your Wall Record is here" — recommended
Plain delivery. They just asked for this thing sixty seconds ago; the job of
this subject line is to be findable in the inbox, not clever. Matches the
voice profile's calm register.

### B: "Write down the width today"
Action-first, creates mild urgency without manufacturing it. Bolder, but risks
reading as instruction before trust exists.

### C: "The one number worth writing down"
Curiosity play. Highest open potential, weakest findability later when they
search their inbox for the sheet.

**Recommended A/B test:** A vs C
**Reason:** Tests plain-delivery findability against curiosity. For a
transactional delivery email, A should win on trust; if C wins meaningfully,
the audience is more curiosity-driven than the calm-authority voice assumes —
useful signal for every later email.

## Preview Text
"Plus the one measurement worth taking today, before you forget."

## Send Timing
Day 0 — immediately after opt-in.
Delivery emails must arrive while the tab is still open. Any delay on a

## Email Copy

Here's your Wall Record.

**[ Download the Wall Record (PDF) ]**

It's one page. Print it, or keep it on your phone — either works.

**Do this part today, while you're thinking about it:**

Measure the crack at its widest point and write the number down, with the date.
Then take a photo straight on, with a coin or tape measure next to it for scale.

That's the whole job. Two minutes.

The reason it matters: a crack that hasn't moved in six months is a
fundamentally different problem from one that has. Nobody can tell you which
one you have from a single look — including us. Only the second measurement
answers it, and you can't take the second measurement if you never took the
first.

In about 90 days I'll send you a short reminder to measure again. That's the
main thing this list is for.

Between now and then you'll get a handful of emails about reading stucco —
what the patterns mean, what's actually behind a wall, and what we've learned
about why repairs come back. If that's not useful, there's an unsubscribe link
at the bottom of every one and no hard feelings.

One question, if you have ten seconds: **which pattern did you land on in the
guide?** Just reply with the number. I read them.

— Daytona Beach Stucco Company

---

**P.S.** If you landed on Pattern 4 — staining, soft spots, bubbling paint, or
white chalky deposits along the crack — don't wait on the 90-day reminder.
That's the one where time actually matters. Reply and say so.
