## Email Copy

Everything in this sequence so far has been about the limits of looking at a
wall. This one is about what happens when you stop looking and start measuring.

A **moisture meter** does one job: it reports how much moisture is present in a
material, at a specific spot, at a specific moment. Two kinds get used on
stucco:

**Pinless meters** read through the surface using electrical impedance. Nothing
penetrates the finish. They're fast, which makes them good for scanning a wall
and comparing one area against another.

**Pin-type or probe meters** take a reading at depth through small penetrations.
Slower, more invasive, and more specific about *where* in the assembly the
moisture is sitting — which is often the thing that matters.

What makes either useful isn't a single number. It's **comparison.** A reading
under a window means very little on its own. That same reading against a
reading from a dry section of the same wall, on the same day, in the same
conditions, means quite a lot. That's the difference between a data point and
a diagnosis.

We also use a **thermal camera** on some jobs. Worth being precise about what
it does, because it gets oversold: a thermal camera shows temperature
differences across a surface. Damp areas often read cooler as they evaporate —
so the camera is good at saying *"something's different here, look closer."*

It does not prove water is present. Temperature differences have other causes:
a stud behind the wall, a gap in insulation, sun on one side of the house an
hour ago. Anyone who tells you their camera found the water is telling you
something a camera cannot do. It points; the meter answers. That's why we use
it when it's appropriate rather than on every job.

Which brings up the honest limit on our end, the same way the guide had one:

**We can't tell you whether there's moisture in your wall without going to your
wall.** Not from a photo, not from a description, not over the phone. The
readings have to come from your specific wall, in your specific conditions, at
specific tested locations. That's just what the measurement is.

Next email I'll lay out exactly what that looks like when we do it — the steps,
what it costs, and what you end up holding at the end. No surprises.
