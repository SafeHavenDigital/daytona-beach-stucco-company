# Email 6: Soft Pitch

## Subject Line Variants

### A: "What the Stucco Moisture Check is, and what it costs" — recommended
Names the offer and the fact that it costs money, in the subject line. In a
Stage 3 low-trust market, leading with price transparency is the trust move —
and it filters for people genuinely willing to pay for a diagnostic.

### B: "$300 to $500, and here's exactly what for"
Price-forward. Bolder, likely lower opens but higher intent among openers.

### C: "The part where I tell you what we charge"
Self-aware and warm. Slight risk of reading as apologetic about the price,
which undercuts a diagnostic that should feel worth paying for.

**Recommended A/B test:** A vs B
**Reason:** Tests whether naming the number in the subject helps or hurts.
Result is directly reusable on the landing page and in ads.

## Email Copy

Five emails in, I haven't asked you for anything. Here's the ask, and I want to
be straightforward about all of it.

We sell a diagnostic service called the **Stucco Moisture Check.** It costs
**$300 to $500**, depending on the size and scope of the evaluation.

**What happens:**

A visual examination of the walls in question, then moisture readings taken
with pinless and/or pin-type probe meters at locations chosen because the
readings there will actually mean something — around openings, below problem
areas, and in comparable dry areas for reference. Where it's appropriate, we
use thermal imaging to identify areas worth testing more closely.

**What you get:**

- Documented findings
- Relevant photos
- Moisture readings from the areas tested
- An explanation of what those results mean
- Recommended next steps

**Now the parts most companies would leave out.**

**This is not a free estimate.** Our repair estimates are free, the same as
everyone else's. The Moisture Check is a separate paid service. It is not
bundled into an estimate, and it is not a bonus that comes with a quote.

**The fee is not credited toward repair work.** If you hire us for the repair
afterward, the Moisture Check still costs what it costs. I know some companies
credit a diagnostic fee toward the job. We don't, and the reason is that a
credited fee quietly turns the diagnosis into a sales call — there's pressure
on the finding to justify the work. Paying for it outright is what keeps the
answer honest, including when the answer is "your wall is fine."

**It is not a home inspection.** In Florida, home inspectors are a separately
licensed profession, and this is not that. This is a stucco contractor's
moisture assessment of your walls. It's the right tool for this specific
question and it is not a substitute for a home inspection or an independent
third-party report.

**You can take the findings anywhere.** They're yours. Get repair quotes from
whoever you like. Several people have used the documentation to have a
conversation with a contractor who'd already quoted them a patch.

**Who this is actually for:**

Pattern 4 from the guide — staining, soft spots, bubbling paint, efflorescence,
a musty smell against the wall inside. Also worth it if you're buying or
selling and need to know what you're dealing with before a number gets
negotiated.

**Who it isn't for:** Pattern 1 and Pattern 2 with no staining and no
softness. Take the measurement, use the Wall Record, look again in six months.
That's a real answer, not a brush-off.
