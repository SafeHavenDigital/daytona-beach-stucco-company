# Campaign: Stucco Triage Guide

## Goal
Capture the pre-diagnosis homeowner — the person who has seen a crack and does
not yet know whether it matters — and convert the subset with moisture symptoms
into paid Stucco Moisture Check bookings. Secondary goal: rank for the
`hairline cracks in stucco when to worry` cluster, rated `~high` volume with
rich PAA in `keyword-plan.md`, which currently has no local competitor serving it.

Metric: Moisture Check bookings attributed to the guide. Secondary: Wall Record
opt-ins.

## Format
Guide — visual/diagnostic triage guide, 4 patterns with verdicts.
**Hybrid delivery:** full guide ungated on-page; the Wall Record tracking sheet
and 90-day reminder are email-gated.

## Hook
"What Your Stucco Crack Is Actually Telling You: A Homeowner's Guide to Knowing
When to Worry"

⚠️ **Hook is hypothesis-driven — see Open Gaps.**

## Target Audience
Volusia County residential homeowners with visible stucco cracking who do not
yet know whether it is serious. Problem-aware, solution-unaware. Per
`audience.md` Q1 `[OWNER]`: they arrive undiagnosed and are outsourcing the
diagnosis.

**Segment A only, by explicit client decision 2026-08-30.** This is a
first-magnet targeting choice and does NOT narrow the business audience, which
remains residential homeowners, commercial property owners, realtors, condo/HOA
boards, and property managers.

## Bridge to Paid Offer
The guide resolves Patterns 1–3 honestly and by design **cannot** resolve
Pattern 4. Surface symptoms — staining, softness, bubbling, efflorescence — are
outside evidence of an inside condition, and the only way to answer it is a
moisture reading taken against the wall.

The bridge is structural rather than persuasive: the guide runs out of road at
exactly the point the paid service begins. Readers in Patterns 1–2 are told
plainly they do not need to buy anything, which is what makes the Pattern 4
recommendation credible.

Pattern 3 deliberately routes AWAY from the Moisture Check toward a structural
engineer where movement is indicated. Do not "fix" this — it is the proof that
the guide is triage and not a funnel.

## Paid Offer
**Stucco Moisture Check — $300–$500**, depending on size and scope.

Hard constraints observed in the copy (all from `positioning.md`, verified
2026-08-28):
- Separate PAID service. Never conflated with the free estimate.
- Fee is NOT credited toward repair work.
- NOT a formal home inspection; "inspection/inspector" framing avoided
  throughout. Florida licenses home inspectors separately.
- NOT an independent third-party report. Deliverable called findings/results.
- Thermal imaging presented as conditional ("where appropriate") and as a
  screening aid that points to test locations — never as proof of moisture.
- Meters named as pin-type probe or pinless, per verified process.
- Hedged language preserved deliberately, per positioning note.

## Competitive Differentiation
Research 2026-08-30. Three local competitors (FCC Builders, Amazing Stucco,
Liberty Stucco) offer **no lead magnet at all** — every one converts on "free
estimate." National stucco assets are uniformly *maintenance* checklists for
walls that are fine (The Stucco Guy, Stucco Champions).

**Nobody serves triage.** The gap is the moment between "I see a crack" and "I
need to call someone."

Structural advantage: inspection companies (Stucco Safe, $495–$1,595) own
diagnosis but cannot repair; contractors repair but will not diagnose. This
company does both, and at $300–$500 sits below Stucco Safe's floor. Requires no
license number to assert — important, since none is available.

## Visual Assets — REQUIRED BEFORE PUBLICATION

**No real project or crack photography exists as of 2026-08-30.** Client
confirmed photography will be revisited later; initial visuals may be
AI-generated, with real photos added or substituted later.

**Binding constraints on visuals:**

1. **AI-generated imagery must NEVER be presented as real customer work, real
   project documentation, or proof of past jobs.** No captions such as "a recent
   Daytona Beach job," no implication of provenance, no before/after framing
   that implies a real repair.
2. Acceptable use of AI imagery here is **illustrative/diagrammatic only** —
   showing what a crack *pattern* looks like in the abstract, functioning as a
   labeled diagram.
3. Preferred alternative: **line diagrams or illustrations**, which carry no
   risk of being mistaken for documentation and suit a triage guide better than
   photographs anyway.
4. Any AI-generated visual must carry a visible label identifying it as an
   illustration.
5. **Real project photography, when available, is the substitution target** and
   is materially stronger — particularly for Pattern 4, where staining and
   efflorescence are hard to illustrate convincingly.

**The guide was written to work without photographs.** Every pattern is
identified by measurable, describable criteria — width against a credit card
(≈0.76 mm) or dime (≈1.35 mm) edge, direction, location, accompanying symptoms
— so a reader can self-identify with a ruler and no images at all. Visuals
enhance it; they are not load-bearing. This also means later photo substitution
requires no rewrite.

# Campaign: Stucco Triage Guide — Welcome Sequence

**Appended 2026-08-30 by /email-sequences.** The lead-magnet brief above is
unmodified; this section covers the email sequence built on it.

## Goal
Convert Wall Record opt-ins into paid Stucco Moisture Check bookings, and
fulfill the 90-day measurement reminder the lead magnet explicitly promises.

Metric: Moisture Check bookings attributed to the sequence. Secondary: reply
rate on the pattern-identification question (email 1), which doubles as
segmentation data.

Welcome — 8 emails (7 core + 1 long-delay reminder).

## Angle
"Find the Water First" (`positioning.md`). The sequence is one continuous
argument: the outside of a wall is limited evidence about the inside, so the
question worth answering first is whether there is water in the wall.

## Audience Segment
Segment A — Volusia County residential homeowners, problem-aware and
solution-unaware. B2C timing model, Eastern time, weekend sends where the
email asks them to go look at something.

## Lead Magnet
The Stucco Triage Guide (ungated) + the Wall Record (gated). See brief above.

## Paid Offer
Stucco Moisture Check, $300–$500.

## Bridge Logic
Emails 2–5 build the case that surface inspection cannot answer the moisture
question. Email 6 presents the service as the answer to a question already
established, not as a new proposition. Email 7 concedes that waiting is
correct for Patterns 1–2.

**Pattern 3 routing is preserved throughout** (client instruction 2026-08-30):
no email redirects structural-movement readers toward the Moisture Check.

## Timeline
Days 0–18 core, plus day 90. Deliberately slower than the skill's default
0/2/4/6/8/10/12 welcome cadence.

**Rationale:** the skill's own rule — medium price ($100–500) starts selling
after 5–7 value emails. First mention of the paid service is day 14, email 6.
The stretched spacing also suits a considered home-repair purchase; a
contractor emailing every 48 hours reads as desperate.

specifies: `stack.md`, `.env`, MCP servers. `.env` contains only
`RESEND_API_KEY` — transactional-only, not an ESP. No Mailchimp, ConvertKit,
HubSpot, SendGrid, or ActiveCampaign key. No email-capable MCP server.

Output is copy-paste `.md` per the skill's no-ESP branch. **No API automation
