# Stucco moisture testing: finding the water before you pay to close the wall

If your stucco is stained, soft, bubbling, or cracking in the same place it cracked last time, the question worth answering isn't *what will it cost to patch*. It's whether there's water behind the wall.

You can't tell from the outside. That's the whole problem. A wall can look sound from the street and have wet sheathing behind it, and a patch over that is a repair you pay for twice.

Moisture testing answers that question before anyone starts cutting.

## What the Stucco Moisture Check is

It's a paid diagnostic service — **$300–$500, depending on the size and scope of the evaluation.** Three steps:

1. **We look at the wall.** A visual examination for stains, cracks, deterioration, and other signs of possible water intrusion.
2. **We test for moisture.** Pin-type/probe or pinless moisture meters, used to investigate possible hidden moisture behind the stucco.
3. **We scan when it helps.** We own a thermal camera and use it when the wall calls for it, to identify suspicious areas worth testing further.

That third step needs a caveat most people selling it won't give you, and we'll give it while holding the camera: **thermal imaging doesn't prove moisture.** A thermal camera shows temperature differences. Moisture is one thing that causes them. Shade, insulation gaps, and a wall that's been in the sun all afternoon cause them too. The camera points us toward areas worth testing. The meters confirm what's actually there.

There's a second limitation worth knowing, because it affects how we test. Stucco has wire lath embedded in it, and that metal interferes with a pinless meter's field. It's why probe readings — small holes, real numbers — are what settle the question on a stucco wall, and why anyone who tells you they scanned your house with a camera and found moisture is telling you less than they're implying.

### What you get

- Documented findings
- Moisture readings from the areas we tested
- Relevant photos
- An explanation of what the results mean
- Recommended next steps

You get the findings whether or not you hire us for the repair.

This is our own assessment of your stucco as the contractor who performs the work. It isn't a home inspection, and it isn't an independent inspection report.

## When it's worth paying for

Most of the time, cracking in a Daytona Beach wall is cosmetic. Hairline stuff, settling, the ordinary movement of a house on the coast. **You don't need a moisture check for a hairline crack in an otherwise dry wall.** Save your money.

It's worth testing when:

- There are **stains** on the wall, or on the inside of that same wall
- Paint is **bubbling or peeling** in a patch rather than across the whole elevation
- A spot sounds **hollow** or gives slightly under hand pressure
- A crack **keeps coming back** after it's been repaired
- Cracks radiate from **window or door corners**, especially with staining below
- An **inspection flagged** the stucco, or you're selling and want to know before a buyer's inspector does
- A repair quote seems high and you want to know whether the scope is real

That last one is the honest reason a lot of people call. Three contractors gave three numbers and one of them wants to open the wall. Testing tells you which one was right.

## What the numbers mean

Moisture meters give a percentage. What counts as high isn't a code number, but there are widely published conventions, and industry sources land in roughly the same place:

| Reading | What it generally indicates |
|---|---|
| ~8–15% | Ordinary range for an exterior wall |
| ~16–19% | Elevated — worth acting on before damage develops |
| 20%+ | Excessive; damage likely, if not now then over time |
| 25%+ | Critical |

Those bands come from third-party inspection and restoration sources, not from us, and they're a guide rather than a verdict. A single number doesn't decide anything on its own. Where the reading was taken matters, what the wall is built from matters, and what the weather did last week matters. A 19% under a window that leaked in a storm three days ago means something different from a steady 19% on a shaded north wall in a dry month.

What we do with them is tell you straight: here's what we read, here's where, here's what we think it means, here's what I'd do about it.

## What happens if we find water

Then you have a real decision instead of a guess, and the finding usually falls into one of three shapes.

**Localized and recent.** Water is getting in at a specific detail — a window head, a failed sealant joint, a crack that opened up. Fix the entry point, repair the affected section, done. This is the common one and it's the cheapest outcome.

**Localized but old.** Water's been sitting long enough that the lath and sheathing behind the stucco need attention too. Closing that up without addressing it is exactly how the same repair comes back. Bigger job, and the reason testing before you buy a patch is worth $300.

**Widespread.** Enough of the wall assembly has failed that patching it is throwing money at it. That's a re-stucco conversation, not a repair conversation.

We'll tell you which one you have. If it's the first, we'll say so, even though the third one is the bigger invoice.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if there is water behind my stucco?
From the outside, usually you can't. Stains, bubbling paint, soft or hollow-sounding spots, and cracks that keep returning are all reasons to suspect it — but none of them prove it, and a wall can look fine from the street with wet sheathing behind it. Testing is what tells you.

### Does moisture testing damage the stucco?
Probe testing leaves small holes, roughly 3/16" to 1/4", placed in inconspicuous spots such as under windows. They're sealed afterward. Pinless scanning leaves nothing behind at all, but it's less definitive on stucco because the embedded wire lath interferes with the reading.

### Can you find water behind stucco without opening the wall?
Yes — that's the point of testing. Meters and probes read moisture through small sealed holes rather than removing sections of the wall. Opening the wall is what you do *after* you know where to open it.

### What moisture reading is too high?
Roughly 8–15% is an ordinary range for exterior walls, 16–19% is elevated, and 20% or above is excessive with damage likely over time. Those are published industry conventions rather than a code standard, and a reading gets interpreted alongside where it was taken and what the wall is made of.

### Does thermal imaging prove there's moisture behind stucco?
No, and we own one. A thermal camera shows temperature differences, and moisture is only one cause of those. We use it to identify suspicious areas worth testing further — that's real work, and it's how we decide where the probes go. It just isn't proof on its own. The meters confirm moisture.

### Is this the same as a home inspection?
No. It's our own assessment of your stucco, as the contractor who does the work. It isn't a home inspection and it isn't an independent inspection report. If you need an independent report — for a lender, or a legal matter — you want a licensed inspector, not us.

### Is the estimate free?
The repair estimate is free. The Stucco Moisture Check is not — it's $300–$500, paid on its own. Those are two separate things and we keep them separate on purpose.
